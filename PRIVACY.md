# Sybl privacy policy

Last updated: 29 October 2026

Sybl is an app that shows what is due in your classes. This page says what it
collects, why, where it goes, how long it is kept, and what you can do about it.

## Who is responsible

Nikko makes Sybl and is responsible for your information.
Write to privacy@sybl.ca with any
question, request or complaint about it.

Sybl is not made by, or connected with, BCIT or D2L.

## What stays on your computer

Sybl keeps your classes, ticks, notes, projects and course posts on your computer,
with your calendar address and, once you sign in, your email address. It makes a
backup copy each day it runs and keeps ten, one for each of the last ten days it
ran; the copies hold all of this, your calendar address too. Your sign-in is kept
in your computer's own password store, Windows Credential Manager or the macOS
Keychain. It also keeps a
log to help fix problems, which never holds your calendar address, your email
address or a sign-in code. None of this is sent anywhere, except as this page says.
When another account signs in to Sybl on a computer, Sybl deletes the last
account's projects from it.

Sybl reads your calendar, and your course posts if you add them, straight from the
Learning Hub. The Learning Hub sees those requests, as it does when a calendar app
reads your calendar.

## What your account holds

You need an account to use Sybl. Asking for a sign-in code creates your account
straight away, even if you never type the code in. It keeps a copy of what you
did, so it is safe and reaches your other computers. It holds:

- your email address, which is how you sign in;
- what you did to your items, when, and on which of your computers: ticks,
  notes, deletions, your changes to a title, date or type, items you added, and
  classes you archived;
- your calendar address, so that your other computer finds your calendar by
  itself;
- your projects: the calendar's identifier for each assignment in them, ticked or
  not; the tasks you plan, with who does each, when it is due and how far along
  it is; the names of the classmates you add; your links, with the names you give
  them; and your notes;
- the computers you signed in on: whether each is Windows or a Mac, its name on
  Windows (a Mac normally sends none), when you first signed in there and when it
  last connected. Each sign-in stays on this list until you delete your account,
  even after you sign out.

Your calendar address works like a password: anyone who has it can read your whole
calendar. Sybl only ever sends it back to computers signed in to your account. It
is stored in the account database as it is, so the person who looks after that
database, named above, could see it. If you think someone else has it, get a new
calendar address from the Learning Hub.

Only you can see your projects. Sybl does not share them, and does not tell the
classmates you name, so add only what they are happy for you to keep. A project you hide is kept like the others, until you delete
your account. Sybl never sends a project's name, but a project it finds by itself
is known by an identifier worked out from facts about the course, such as its code
and the dates of its assignments, so it is not a secret: whoever holds the account's
data could work out which course and topic a project is about.

## What Sybl never collects

- What your calendar says about an item you have not changed.
- Anything about your grades.
- Your course posts, or the link they come from.
- Your BCIT password. Sybl never asks for it.
- Your age. Sybl is made for college and university students and does not ask it.

There are no analytics, crash reports, ads, cookies or tracking. We never sell your
information, and never use it to train AI.

## Where it is kept, and who else sees it

- **Your account** is stored in Canada, in Supabase's Canada (Central) region.
  Supabase Pte. Ltd., a company based in Singapore, runs the service on Amazon Web
  Services, with help from the companies it lists at
  <https://supabase.com/legal/subprocessors>. Supabase also sees your computer's
  internet address each time Sybl connects (when it starts, after each change, and
  every 15 minutes while it is open) and keeps it, with a record of each sign-in, in
  its own service logs, to keep the service running and safe. Those logs never hold
  what Sybl sends, only that a request was made. Supabase deletes them by itself
  after a time it sets; they are not part of your account.
- **Sign-in codes** are emailed by Twilio SendGrid, in the United States. It sees
  your email address and the code, and nothing else.
- **Updates** come from GitHub, in the United States. GitHub sees your computer's
  internet address, as it does for any download. Sybl sends nothing about you with
  it.

Information handled in another country can be reached by that country's courts
and authorities.

Nikko can reach the account database to keep the service running,
and looks at what is in it only when you ask for help or the law requires it.

## How long it is kept

- **Your account** is kept until you delete it. An account nobody uses is not
  deleted by itself.
- **The history of your changes** is kept for 90 days, so that your other
  computers can catch up. Older history is removed the next time one of your
  computers connects to your account. If you stop using Sybl, the history stays
  until you delete your account. The latest version of each change is kept until
  then too. That includes an old calendar address after you change it.
- **Twilio SendGrid** keeps a record of each email it sends, with your address, for
  up to 30 days. If an email cannot be delivered, it keeps your address on a
  do-not-send list until we remove it.
- **On your computer**, everything stays until you remove it.

## Your copy, and deleting it

In Sybl, open Settings, then Data and safety:

- **Download my data** saves a file of your changes, their history, your
  computers and your projects, as your account holds them. It includes your
  calendar address and your projects' links, so keep it to yourself.
- **Delete account** deletes your account and everything it holds, your projects
  too, straight away, an account made by asking for a code that was never used too.
  Supabase's service logs go when it deletes them, and the account service's own
  daily backups drop it within seven days. What is on your
  computer stays, except your projects, which Sybl deletes from the computer you
  delete the account on. Your other computers keep their copy of your projects,
  hidden, until another account signs in on them, and each computer's daily
  backups keep them for up to ten days more.

To remove Sybl from a computer, sign out first, which removes your sign-in from it.
On Windows, uninstall Sybl and tick the box to delete its data. On a Mac, delete
Sybl, then delete the AssignmentTrackerV2 folder in your Library's Application
Support folder.

You can also write to us to ask what we hold about you, for a copy of it, or to
correct it, for example to change your email address. We answer within 30 days.

## Automatic updates

Sybl checks for a new version when it starts and once a day, and downloads it.
It installs an update by itself, while its window is closed, only if you allow
automatic updates. It asks you once, when you set it up or in your list of work,
and you can change your answer at any time in Settings, under General. If you do
not allow them, a new version waits there until you press Install.

An update changes only Sybl itself, to fix problems and add features. The request
comes from the person named at the top of this page, who you can write to at any
time.

## If something goes wrong

If your information is lost, stolen or seen by someone who should not see it, and
that could harm you, we will email you as soon as we can to say what happened and
what you can do, and we will report it to the privacy commissioner.

## Questions and complaints

Write to privacy@sybl.ca. We answer within 30 days.

If you are not happy with the answer, you can complain to the Office of the
Information and Privacy Commissioner for British Columbia, at
<https://www.oipc.bc.ca>. If you live outside BC, you can complain to the Office
of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, at <https://www.priv.gc.ca>.

## Changes to this page

If Sybl changes what it collects or where it goes, this page changes first, with a
new date, and the notes for that update say so.
